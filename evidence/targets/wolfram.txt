Independent check of the hello-host output hash. Not computed by the C program.

Program: c[i] = a[i] + b[i] for i = 0 .. 2^20-1
  a[i] = i/4, exact in binary32 for this range
  b[i] = binary32 rounding of 1/(1 + i mod 7)

Binary32 reciprocals (Wolfram, round ties to even), as hex bits:
  1/1  3f800000
  1/2  3f000000
  1/3  3eaaaaab
  1/4  3e800000
  1/5  3e4ccccd
  1/6  3e2aaaab
  1/7  3e124925

Why a binary64 sum rounded to binary32 is the binary32 add here: a[i] and b[i] are binary32 values, so both are exact in binary64. Their exponents differ by at most 21 (2^18 against 1/7), and 21 is under 29, so the sum is exact in binary64. One rounding of that sum to binary32 is the binary32 addition.

Wolfram then exported the 2^20 sums as little-endian Real32 (ExportByteArray, checked: 1.0 is bytes 0,0,128,63) and hashed them.

length: 4194304 bytes (2^20 * 4)
bytes moved by the program: 12582912 (2^20 * 12)
sha256: 72d0c1b2928779e83518f0b4c7d101d933cf89f210cd4627e138c33c9ce9b6a6

That is the hash printed by hello_host on Apple M (arm64), on x86_64, and on the Spark (aarch64).

Copy-probe ratios, recomputed from the measured milliseconds in the three receipts (32 * 2^20 bytes / seconds / 2^30):
  Apple M, 1.016 ms: 30.757874 GiB/s, program printed 30.76
  x86_64, 1.504 ms: 20.777926 GiB/s, program printed 20.78
  Spark CPU, 1.276 ms: 24.490596 GiB/s, program printed 24.50
These probes are not chip ceilings, and they are not a comparison with CUDA.
